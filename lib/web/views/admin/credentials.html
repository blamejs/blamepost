Two ceilings ride on every credential over a rolling window, one on messages and one on recipients, because a hundred messages carrying one recipient each and one message carrying a hundred thousand are different events. A credential at either of them is refused with 452 4.5.3 on submission and 429 over this server's own sending route, and the message is deferred rather than lost: a sending application retries and the window drains on its own.

Both acts on this page are the operator's way of not waiting for that. Giving the window back puts what a credential has sent to zero and leaves its ceilings alone; changing the ceilings moves what it may send and leaves what it has already sent counted. The counters live in this process, so both acts take effect on the credential's next message and both are here rather than on the command line: every blamepost credential verb that writes needs the data volume this server is holding, and stopping the server to reach one destroys the window it was going to give back.

A credential reaching a ceiling it never used to reach is the shape a stolen one makes. Read what it sent before raising anything, and replace its secret with blamepost credential rotate <id> --reason <text> if the traffic is not the application's.
