Hashed with Argon2id before it reaches disk, and checked against the policy first.

Zero leaves the account uncapped.

The account is created without the operator console. To give it the console, press Give it the console on its row once it exists, which asks for a reason.

Suspending stops sign in and submission and leaves the mailbox alone. Archiving closes the account and keeps its mail. Erasing the keys that unseal it is a separate, recorded decision with its own reason, because it cannot be undone. Every one of these asks for a reason before it happens, and archiving asks you to type the address as well, because the row above and the row below look the same from a mouse.

The console is a grant over the whole server rather than over any mailbox: an account that holds it reads every address, every hosted domain, the outbound spool and this trail. Take it back when somebody stops needing it, or when a credential has been taken. Revoking reaches a session that is already open, and it leaves the mailbox working, so it is the narrower answer than suspending an account whose owner did nothing wrong.

An account out of service can have the role taken back and cannot be given it. Both directions of that follow from the same fact: it authenticates nowhere, so a grant on it would sit unused and read as an administrator on every listing until the mailbox came back. Revoking is offered on every row that holds the role because this page is the only place the role is granted or taken, and an account part way through an erasure cannot be returned to service at all, so a row it was not offered on would keep the role for good.
